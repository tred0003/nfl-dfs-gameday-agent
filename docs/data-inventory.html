Three words used throughout

TermPlain English
GrainWhat one row means. "Player-week" = one row per player per game. "Season total" = one row per player per season. The model needs player-week.
Known before kickoffThe one rule for any feature: could you have known it before the game started? If not, the model is peeking at the answer (leakage). A season total used for a game inside that season breaks this rule; last season's total does not.
Fill %The share of rows where a column actually has a value. 65% means 35% are blank.

1 · In the model today

Four sources feed the model. It ranks each week's players with a score of 0.5958 (slate-rank Spearman, 2021–2024) against 0.5791 for a plain rolling average. An honest caveat: those two numbers were measured on different row counts (21,071 vs 16,751), so the true gap is not settled yet.

Snap counts IN MODEL

data/raw/snaps.parquet · 253,106 rows · 2016–2025 · all positions incl. defense and special teams · source: Pro Football Reference via nflverse

offense_pct = the share of his team's offensive plays he was on the field for. The model uses 3- and 5-game averages. Joined by name + team (it uses a different player ID), so a few players get lost in the join.

3 · Free data, downloaded 2026-09-15

All free from nflverse, now in data/raw/. None of it is in the model yet. Current-season (2026) files grow every week; re-run the fetch script with --refresh to update them.

Play-by-play NEW, UNUSED

data/raw/pbp/ · 2016–2026 (2026 = Week 1 so far) · ~47–50k plays a season · 372 columns · 199 MB

Every play: down, distance, field position (yardline_100), pass/run, air yards, who was targeted, result, win probability, EPA. Anything situational can be counted from it:

  • Red-zone and goal-line carries and targets (inside the 20, 10, 5)
  • End-zone targets and deep targets
  • Pass rate over expected by team (does a coach throw more than the situation calls for?)
  • Pace: seconds per play, plays per game
  • 2025 weekly stats, rebuilt to fill the gap

Participation NEW, UNUSED

data/raw/participation/ · 2016–2025 (2026 not published yet) · ~45–51k plays a season · player names and positions added from 2023

The IDs of all 11 offensive and 11 defensive players on the field, plus formation, personnel, defenders in the box, pass rushers, time to throw, pressure, man/zone coverage, and a route label on pass plays.

Weekly routes run, calculated free: the share of team pass plays a receiver was on the field for. Checked against FantasyData's 2025 route participation, it landed within 1–4 points for six top WRs (e.g., Chase 96.5% vs 97.2%, Nacua 78.7% vs 77.2%). Not yet tested for TEs and RBs, where blocking snaps make it looser.

FTN film charting NEW, UNUSED

data/raw/ftn/ · 2022–2026 · ~42–48k plays a season

Human-charted flags on every play: is_catchable_ball, is_contested_ball, is_created_reception, is_drop, is_interception_worthy, is_play_action, is_motion, is_rpo, is_screen_pass, n_blitzers, is_qb_out_of_pocket, is_throw_away.

This is the weekly, free version of several FantasyData film stats (contested catches, catchable-ball rate, interception-worthy throws).

Next Gen Stats (weekly) NEW, UNUSED

data/raw/ngs/ · 2016–2026 · receiving 14,877 · rushing 6,127 · passing 5,997 rows (week 0 = season totals)

Receiving: avg_separation (distance from the nearest defender at the catch or incompletion), avg_cushion (how far off the defender plays at the snap), avg_intended_air_yards, avg_yac_above_expectation. Only the more-involved players each week (~1,350 receiving rows a season). Cushion is the defense's own read on a receiver's speed, making it the best free "losing a step" signal.

Pro Football Reference advanced (weekly) NEW, UNUSED

data/raw/pfr/ · rush, rec, pass, def · 2018–2026 (2026 Week 1 incomplete upstream: 4 teams)

Rushing yards before and after contact, broken tackles, receiving drops and drop %, passing drops, and more. Broken tackles run about one-third of FantasyData's "evaded tackles" (a stricter definition), but in six 2025 backs the top two and bottom one were the same players on both lists.

Draft picks · Combine · Contracts NEW, UNUSED

data/raw/players/ · draft picks 12,927 (1980–2026) · combine 8,968 (2000–2026) · contracts 52,816

Full draft history; 40-yard dash, weight, vertical, etc.; contract value and guaranteed money. A team that just gave a player guaranteed money has a reason to keep using him.

5 · Where an edge could hide

Every idea below is a hypothesis, not a finding. The pattern behind all of them: DraftKings sets salaries days before kickoff, mostly from past points. An edge exists wherever something important changes after the price is set, or sits under the points where a price can't see it. The test is always the same: does it predict points beyond what the current model already knows, in most seasons, not just one?

IdeaWhy salary might miss itData you have for it
Vacated opportunityA starter is ruled out after pricing; his targets and carries go to someone whose salary didn't moveInjuries + depth charts + target share (all on disk)
Role changeA player's routes or snaps jump before his points do. Price follows points, so it lags.Snaps (on disk); participation routes (free)
Touchdown luckA player with lots of red-zone and end-zone targets but few touchdowns is due; the reverse is overpriced. TDs swing year to year; opportunity is steadier.Play-by-play red-zone counts (free); season end-zone targets (FantasyData)
Early-season reputation lagWeek 1–4 prices lean on last year's points, not the efficiency underneathFantasyData season totals 2021–25; NGS, PFR, FTN (free)
Aging / losing a stepA vet whose points held up but whose yards created, separation, or cushion fellAge (crosswalk); yards created (FantasyData); cushion (NGS, free)
Rookies and draft capitalNo history means the model knows little; salary is a guessDraft round/pick (crosswalk); combine (free)
Game environmentHigh over/under games score more at every position (QBs +60% in 52+ totals). Likely priced in already, so it's worth checking how completely.Vegas lines (in model); points by total (measured 2026-09-15)
Game scriptBig favorites run 4 more times a game, but don't pass less. Fading their WRs loses (9.8 vs 7.7 pts vs. big underdogs).Spread (in model); measured 2026-09-15
Coach and referee tendenciesPace and pass rate differ by coach; penalty rates by crew. Salaries rarely account for either.Coach and referee columns (schedules, unused); play-by-play (free)
Beat the paid projectionNot an edge by itself; it's the scoreboard. If the model can't rank players better than FantasyData, it has no edge yet.FantasyData projections 2021–2025 (on disk)

Already tested (FEATURES.md §9)

  • Buy low on TEs after a bad game: best-supported finding in the project. Don't buy low on RBs (a dud RB is usually hurt or benched). WRs: neutral.
  • Fade WR1s with a green QB: your original rule, inverted by the data.
  • Wind changes play-calling but not RB points. Rain is untested (no data).
  • The bounce-back rule may be right in points per dollar but invisible in points. That test needs the salary data you now have.
